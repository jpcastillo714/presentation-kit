How to find a planet you cannot see

Two tiny signals: the shadow and the wobble

The background spectrum sways: this is, greatly exaggerated, a star moving towards and away from us.

The shadow

When a planet passes in front, its star dims a little

Depth = (R_planet / R_star)², with IAU (2015) nominal radii: Jupiter in front of the Sun, 1.06 %; Earth, 84 ppm. No limb darkening; Earth's size not to scale in the curve.

First a Jupiter-sized planet: the star loses 1 % of its light. Next, an Earth-sized one: 84 parts per million, and the vertical scale changes so it can be seen.
The light curve is drawn while the planet crosses: cause and effect together (temporal contiguity, F25). The change of scale is stated on the axis, not hidden (G10).

The wobble

The star wobbles too: Earth moves the Sun by only 9 cm/s

Kepler's third law, circular orbit: Earth induces 8.9 cm/s on the Sun; Jupiter, 12.5 m/s. Motion and shift exaggerated so they can be seen.

Step 1: the star also orbits the center of mass. Step 2: seen edge-on, it approaches and recedes. Step 3: the spectral lines shift to the blue and the red at the same pace.
The measurement comes out of the motion in plain sight (F29: congruence). One new idea per step.

The trick

A single line is noise; adding thousands, the error drops as 1/√N

Simulation with identical Gaussian lines and photon noise; these are not real lines. The velocity error drops as 1/√N: with 2000 lines, about 45 times smaller.

This is how centimeters per second are measured: no single line is enough, but thousands together are.
Accumulating is a process: the animation shows it as it happens, and the 1/√N rule appears after it has been seen (example before rule, F11 and F12).

What is measured

Together, the two signals tell what the planet is made of

  • The shadow gives the size: the depth is (R_planet / R_star)².
  • The wobble gives the mass (the minimum: it depends on the orbit's inclination).
  • Size and mass give the density: rock like Earth or gas like Jupiter.

Standard relations of the transit and radial-velocity methods.

After four slides in motion, a still one: this is where it all comes together.
A still slide after the animated run marks the change of pace (principles, "Two registers"). The three ideas are revealed one at a time.

The scale

Finding another Earth means signals more than a hundred times smaller than a Jupiter's

Own computation: velocity (Kepler) 12.5 m/s vs 8.9 cm/s, ÷139; depth (IAU 2015) 1.06 % vs 84 ppm, ÷126. Logarithmic scales.

First Jupiter on both scales; next, Earth slides to its place and the ratio for each signal appears.
Sliding along a logarithmic scale is a change of scale: the distance is felt by travelling it (F23). Position on a common scale (G20).
