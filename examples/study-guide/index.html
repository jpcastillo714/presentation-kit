Why averaging works, and when it stops working

Two pages and two simulators to study the 1/√N rule before an exam or a lab session.

The average of N independent measurements scatters √N times less than one measurement. Averaging removes what changes from one measurement to the next, never what stays the same.

  1. 1. The average scatters √N times lessWhy 4 times more data only halves the error
  2. 2. Averaging cannot remove an offsetWhere the 1/√N rule stops: the systematic floor

Every number on these pages comes from the simulators, which draw random measurements with the spread and offset you choose; there is no real dataset. The result σ/√N assumes independent measurements with the same spread.
